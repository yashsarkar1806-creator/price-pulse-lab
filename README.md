# Price Pulse Lab

**Live demo:** https://yashsarkar1806-creator.github.io/price-pulse-lab/?demo=1

A browser-based pricing research tool for DTC and startup marketers. Upload a pricing survey and get:

- **An acceptable price range** from the Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (point of marginal cheapness, optimal price point, indifference price point, point of marginal expensiveness), with the six classic curves charted.
- **A purchase-intent and revenue curve** from Gabor-Granger questions, including revenue and contribution per 100 respondents, the revenue- and profit-maximising price, and arc elasticity between price steps.
- **Segment cuts** (for example students vs. coffee enthusiasts) so you can see who will pay what.
- **A plain-English readout** you can paste into a launch deck, with the honest caveat that stated intent overstates real buying.
- **A suggested live price test**: up to three well-separated, charm-priced cells to A/B test on a landing page, judged on revenue per visitor.

Everything runs client-side. No data leaves the browser, and there is no build step.

## Why I built it

Pricing is one of the biggest growth levers a DTC brand has, but launch prices are often set by gut feel or by copying a competitor. This tool turns a quick survey into a defensible starting range and a concrete test plan, which is how I'd approach pricing a new product as a marketer: research to narrow the range, then a live test to decide.

## Demo data (synthetic)

`data/thanda-synthetic-survey.csv` holds 400 **synthetic** respondents for a **fictional** brand, "Thanda Cold Brew" (monthly 8-pack subscription, priced in INR), across three segments. It is generated by `scripts/generate-synthetic.js` with a fixed seed and includes a few deliberately illogical answers so the cleaning step has something to do. It is not real customer data and the results are illustrative only.

## Use your own survey

CSV columns (headers are case-insensitive):

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `too_cheap` | Price so low you'd doubt the quality |
| `cheap` | Price that feels like a bargain |
| `expensive` | Price that feels expensive but you'd still consider |
| `too_expensive` | Price too high to consider |
| `buy_<price>` (optional, any number) | 1/0 or yes/no: would you buy at this price? e.g. `buy_799` |
| `segment` (optional) | Any grouping label |

Respondents whose four Van Westendorp answers aren't in logical order are dropped and counted.

## Run locally

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000/?demo=1
npm test                      # 10 unit tests for the pricing maths (Node 18+)
```

## Files

- `pricing.js`: pure pricing maths (CSV parsing, cleaning, PSM curves and intersections, Gabor-Granger, readout, test cells), usable in the browser or Node.
- `app.js`, `index.html`, `styles.css`: the interface and canvas charts.
- `test/pricing.test.js`: unit tests.

## Methods

- Van Westendorp, P. (1976), "NSS Price Sensitivity Meter: A new approach to study consumer perception of prices."
- Gabor, A. & Granger, C. W. J. (1966), "Price as an indicator of quality."
